## Arabic delivery, without an interpreter

Most international AI training reaching Doha arrives in English, built for a Western corporate
context. Ahmed Amrousy delivers verbally in Arabic, from material he wrote himself, so questions are
asked and answered in the room's own language. Slides and take-away documents stay in English, which
is what most Qatari corporate environments use day to day — and full-English delivery is available
for the mixed teams that are the norm in Doha.

## You already have a law. The session maps to it.

Qatar has a general personal data protection law, Law No. 13 of 2016, which was the first of its
kind in the Gulf. Organisations licensed inside the Qatar Financial Centre fall under the QFC's own
data protection regulations instead.

That makes Qatar unusual among the markets Menova serves. In most of them, "what can we paste into
an AI tool?" has no external answer and the first session has to write one. Here the obligation
already exists, so the session does something more useful: it maps the team's actual intended
usage — the documents they want to summarise, the records they want to analyse — against the regime
that applies to them, and marks which of those are safe, which need anonymising first, and which
cannot leave the organisation at all. Your counsel confirms the legal position; the session makes it
operational for people who are not lawyers.

## Built for the Qatar National Vision 2030 agenda

Qatar adopted a national AI strategy built on pillars including education, data access, employment,
business, research and ethics, with an AI committee established to coordinate it. For a leadership
team, that turns into practical questions: which functions to move first, what to fund this year,
and how to stop pilots multiplying without ever scaling. The AI for Business Strategy workshop is
built to answer exactly that, using SOSTAC for structure and Playing to Win to force the choices.
