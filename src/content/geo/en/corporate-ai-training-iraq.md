## Arabic delivery, without an interpreter

Most international AI training reaching Iraq arrives in English, built for a Western corporate
context, and is often relayed through a translator. Ahmed Amrousy delivers verbally in Arabic, from
material he wrote himself, so questions are asked and answered directly in the room. Slides and
take-away documents stay in English, which is what most Iraqi corporate and government environments
use for written work.

He speaks Egyptian Arabic — the variety most widely understood across the Arab world — and
participants ask in Iraqi Arabic. There is no interpreter in the middle, which is the part that
usually costs a training day its momentum.

## The gap between the strategy and the room

Iraq announced work on a national AI strategy, INSAIN, in 2024, under a supreme committee for
artificial intelligence chaired by the prime minister, alongside a wider digital transformation
agenda. Public reporting on its current status is inconsistent, and commentary on Iraq's AI
readiness consistently points to the same gap: the agenda is ahead of infrastructure and skills.

That gap is the practical problem for a leadership team. The questions that follow are not about
policy — which functions to change first, what to fund this year, how to stop pilots multiplying
without ever scaling. The AI for Business Strategy workshop is built to answer exactly those, using
SOSTAC for structure and Playing to Win to force the choices.

## Your data rules, written down first

Iraq has no comprehensive data protection law applying to private-sector organisations. Legal
commentary describes a patchwork: public-sector rules, draft legislation that has not passed, and
courts falling back on the Civil Code and Penal Code. For most organisations, the question "what can
we paste into an AI tool?" therefore has no external answer.

Every Menova programme opens by writing that answer down: what is safe to use, what must be
anonymised, and what never leaves the building. For organisations handling citizen data, oil
contracts, or donor and beneficiary records, that session is usually the one the legal team cares
about most.
