## Most "AI adoption" never reaches the second week

An individual discovers a useful prompt, uses it for a fortnight, then reverts. Nothing is written
down, nothing is owned, and the department's actual throughput never changes.

**AI for Automation** treats recurring work as a systems problem rather than a personal-productivity
one. The output is not enthusiasm — it is documented workflows with named owners.

## What gets built

Participants map their department's recurring work on the first morning and rank it honestly.
Frequently a third of what a team wants to automate turns out to be work that should simply stop.
What remains gets rebuilt, tested and documented well enough that a new joiner could run it.

## The verification discipline

An automated process that is wrong 5% of the time and unchecked is worse than the manual one it
replaced. A full module is spent designing the check that catches the specific error the department
can least afford, and drawing the line where a human must decide.
