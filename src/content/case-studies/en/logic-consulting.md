## Where the stakes are different

In most organisations, an unverified AI output produces an internal embarrassment. In a consulting
practice it produces a wrong recommendation in front of a paying client — and the firm's credibility
is the entire asset.

That changes what the training has to emphasise. Speed is the easy part; every participant finds it
within an hour. The discipline is the hard part.

## Verification as a professional obligation

Chain-of-verification was taught as a required step in producing any client-facing analysis, not as
an advanced technique for the interested. A consultant who cannot say where a figure came from
cannot defend it in the room.

## The confidentiality question

Client material carries confidentiality obligations that predate any of these tools. The boundary
session dealt with this directly: what may never be entered into a public AI tool, how to work on
sensitive engagements without exposing client data, and when AI-assisted work should be disclosed to
the client.
