## Why this engagement is worth reading

Most Copilot training is a feature tour. This was the opposite: two days engineered around the
documents a national electricity company actually produces — tenders, budget variances, compliance
matrices, board packs — with the prompting discipline taught first and the tools second.

## Day one: judgement before tools

The first day made no assumptions. It opened with what AI genuinely can and cannot do, took the
room through POCAB for single prompts and GCSE-F for document work, then went deep on the failure
mode that matters most in an energy company: hallucination. Participants learned the four types —
factual, temporal, contextual, linguistic — why they happen, and the verification workflow that
catches them: check the source, distrust confidence without proof, verify names, dates and
numbers, and ask where every claim came from.

The day closed with each executive building a personal ten-prompt toolkit for their own role —
tested and compared in the room, ready to use the next morning.

## Day two: the Copilot full stack

Seven modules, each anchored to a live Kahraba use case:

- **Word** — converting the corporate profile into a one-page management summary; summarising a
  real tender document into technical, commercial and timeline requirements for the bid committee.
- **Excel** — a budget-variance dashboard with KPI cards and charts from the actual variance
  sheets; a vendor-registration compliance matrix extracted clause-by-clause from tender text.
- **Outlook** — thread summaries and drafted replies with executive tone control.
- **PowerPoint** — a 10–12 slide company pitch deck and an executive budget-variance presentation,
  built to "state the conclusion in the slide title".
- **Teams** — chat and meeting summarisation with decisions and action items extracted.
- **Power Automate** — a showcase of AI workflows for recurring processes.
- **AI & data governance** — what may and may not enter the tools, closed with the enterprise
  security conversation a critical-infrastructure company needs.

## The pattern that made it stick

Leadership first, real material throughout, verification built into every exercise. That sequence —
not the tool list — is why the programme rated 4.8 out of 5 and why the same group later returned
for an advanced session on delegated AI work.
