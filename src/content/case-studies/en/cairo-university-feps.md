## The conversation faculty were actually having

Not "should we allow this". Students were already using it. The real question was what an assessment
is for once the output can be generated in ninety seconds, and what a department says to its
students in the first week of term.

## Why the detection question had to come first

Every faculty AI session runs into the same hope: that a detection tool will make the problem go
away. It will not. Detection tools produce false positives against non-native English writers at
uncomfortable rates, and a policy built on unreliable evidence produces contested cases the
department will lose.

Naming that early is uncomfortable, and it is the only honest place to start.

## Peer to peer, not vendor to audience

Ahmed teaches executive education at the American University in Cairo's Onsi Sawiris School of
Business, so the session ran as a conversation between people who teach — not as an external
briefing. The collaboration with the Egyptian Observatory for the Digital Economy placed the
workshops in the wider context of Egypt's digital economy agenda.
