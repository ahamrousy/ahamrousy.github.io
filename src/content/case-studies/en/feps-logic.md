## A faculty workshop built on faculty work

"Mastering AI for Smarter Teaching" was designed around what university teaching actually
consists of — tutorial questions, seminar design, reading lists, accreditation paperwork, datasets,
marking — rather than around AI features. Logic Consulting partnered on the delivery; the room was
FEPS faculty; the examples were economics and political science throughout.

## Verification before anything else

Academic work has no tolerance for invented citations. The workshop's first discipline was
verification-first prompting: forcing the model to expose what is unsupported before any output
enters teaching or research. That discipline then ran through everything else — the GCSE-F
research-synthesis pattern requires "not stated" rather than inference and a citation for every
cell of an evidence table.

## Course design with POCAB, on real courses

Not "write me discussion questions" but a full brief: a teaching assistant persona, six tutorial
questions on exchange-rate pass-through, a 50-minute tutorial of 25 mixed-ability students, no
calculus, Egyptian examples from the last five years, and a flag on any question needing data the
students do not have. Faculty then wrote the same pattern for their own modules — including a
few-shot version that clones the style of two model seminar questions.

## Assignments that survive students having AI

Three redesign patterns, demonstrated on real material: the 1,500-word essay becomes a structured
debate where students use AI to draft both sides and then deconstruct what it produced; the
memorisation question becomes principle-application on the Cairo bread market; the computation
exercise becomes an AI-built demand model the student must interrogate — at what income level does
elasticity flip sign, and what does that do to the subsidy bill?

## The heavy lifting, delegated

The Cowork segment showed what changes when course preparation is delegated rather than assisted:
a syllabus and reading pile returning as a 12-week outline with a question bank; an accreditation
form filled against the syllabus with an ILO matrix and a gap list; fifteen disagreeing PDFs
returning as a cited evidence table plus a two-page conflict memo; a raw dataset returning as a
complete, ready-to-run stats lab — cleaned data with every cleaning decision stated, a graduated
student worksheet, and solutions with the code shown. Draft feedback on anonymised scripts closed
the loop, with the principle stated plainly: you edit, you own.
