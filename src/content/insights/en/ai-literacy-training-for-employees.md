Most organisations in Egypt and the Gulf do not have an AI adoption problem. They have an AI
*visibility* problem. Staff are already pasting documents into free AI tools — on personal phones,
on personal accounts, outside any policy. AI literacy training is how a company turns that from a
risk into a capability.

## What AI literacy training is — and is not

AI literacy training gives every employee a working understanding of generative AI in the context
of their own job. It is not a technical course and it does not produce data scientists. It answers
five practical questions:

1. **What is this tool actually doing?** Enough understanding to know why it sounds confident when
   it is wrong.
2. **What must never go into it?** A written boundary for client data, personal data, financials and
   anything under NDA.
3. **How do I check the output?** Verification habits proportionate to the consequences of being
   wrong.
4. **How do I brief it well?** A repeatable structure, rather than trial and error.
5. **Where does it help *my* work?** Role-specific use cases, built on the participant's own
   documents.

Awareness sessions usually cover the first question and stop. That is why they change so little.

## Why it became urgent

**Unapproved use is already happening.** Banning AI tools does not stop their use; it only stops the
company from seeing it. Training plus a written boundary is the control that actually works.

**For companies with EU exposure, it is now an obligation.** Article 4 of the EU AI Act requires
providers and deployers of AI systems to take measures to ensure a sufficient level of AI literacy
among their staff, and it has applied since 2 February 2025. Egyptian and Gulf organisations with EU
subsidiaries, clients or operations should check with counsel whether it reaches them.

**Local data rules are tightening.** Egypt's Personal Data Protection Law (No. 151 of 2020), Saudi
Arabia's PDPL and Oman's PDPL all make "what did we paste, and where?" a compliance question. See
[the data boundaries guide](/insights/ai-data-boundaries-at-work/) for how to write those rules down.

## What a good programme covers

| Module | What participants leave with |
|---|---|
| How generative AI works, without the jargon | A correct mental model: prediction, not lookup |
| The data boundary | A one-page written rule they can apply without asking IT |
| Verification | When to trust, when to check, when not to use AI at all |
| Briefing the model | Two frameworks — POCAB for drafting, GCSE-F for working from documents ([explained here](/insights/pocab-vs-gcse-f/)) |
| Role-specific practice | Three tasks from their own job, done with AI on anonymised real material |
| Policy and escalation | Who to ask, and what to report |

The last two rows are where literacy turns into use. Training on generic examples produces people
who understand AI in principle and never apply it on Monday.

## Rollout: who goes first

1. **Leadership (1 day).** The people who approve policy and budget must have used the tools on their
   own work. Otherwise the first policy question stalls everything. Menova runs this as
   [AI for Executives](/courses/ai-for-executives/).
2. **Champions (2 days).** One or two people per department who will field their colleagues'
   questions. This is the [16-hour AI for Business workshop](/courses/ai-for-business/).
3. **Everyone else.** Shorter sessions, delivered or supported by the champions, built on the
   department's own use cases.

## What this looks like in practice

At Kahraba, Egypt's largest private-sector power generation and distribution company, Menova trained
65 executives across three runs of a 16-hour AI Foundation Workshop, each run of 20 to 23 people.
Participants rated it 4.8 out of 5. The full engagement is documented in the
[Kahraba case study](/case-studies/kahraba/).

## How to measure it

Attendance and satisfaction scores show the training happened, not that it worked. Measure three
things instead:

- **Capability:** can each participant complete a real task from their own job with AI, unaided?
- **Compliance:** is the written data boundary being followed?
- **Change:** which tasks are done differently thirty days later?

The full method is in [measuring the ROI of AI training](/insights/measuring-roi-of-ai-training/), and
pricing is explained in [what corporate AI training costs](/insights/what-corporate-ai-training-costs/).
