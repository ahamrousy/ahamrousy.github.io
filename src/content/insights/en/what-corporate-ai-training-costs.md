Corporate AI training in Egypt and the Gulf is priced per programme, not per head. The number is
driven by six things: how senior the room is, how many contact hours you book, whether delivery is
in Arabic or English, which city it happens in, how many people are in the room, and how much of the
material is rebuilt around your own work. Travel, venue and paid tool licences sit outside the fee.

That is the whole mechanism. The rest of this page is how to use it.

## Why nobody publishes a price

Search for what AI training costs in this region and you will mostly find contact forms. Some of
that is reasonable — a half-day briefing for eight people and a sixteen-hour strategy workshop for a
board are not the same product, and a single published figure would mislead on both. Some of it is
not reasonable: an unquoted price is harder to argue with.

You do not need a tender to get past it. Four facts are enough for any competent provider to quote:
the number of participants, their seniority, the number of days, and the city. Send those and ask
for a written number. How fast and how clearly that comes back tells you something real about how
the engagement will run.

## The five ways this gets quoted

| Model | How it is quoted | Works when | Watch for |
|---|---|---|---|
| Per programme | One fee for the whole delivery | In-house training for one team | Make sure design and materials are inside the fee |
| Per seat | A price per participant | Public, open-enrolment courses | For in-house work it usually means you are buying seats on a generic course |
| Per contact hour | A rate × hours | Long or phased engagements | Cheap hours are easy to add; check who teaches them |
| Train-the-trainer | A fee to certify your own people | You will run this repeatedly in-house | Quality drops one step away from the source unless the internal trainers are genuinely good |
| Platform subscription | Per user per year for a course library | Self-paced, individual upskilling | This is a library, not training. Completion rates for self-paced corporate content are the thing to ask about |

For a leadership team, the first model is almost always the right one. For individual upskilling of a
large population, the last may be — but it answers a different question, and it will not change how
a management team makes decisions.

## The six things that actually move the number

**Seniority.** A room of department heads needs different preparation from a room of analysts, and
the cost of getting it wrong is higher. Expect senior programmes to be priced above the same number
of hours delivered to a working team.

**Contact hours.** The honest unit. A one-day programme and a two-day programme are not twice apart
in value, because the second day is where people build something; but they are roughly twice apart
in a trainer's committed time.

**What you want at the end.** Capability and a document are different products. "Our managers can
use these tools well" is training. "We leave with a written, sequenced AI plan with owners and kill
criteria" is facilitation, and it needs more hours and more preparation.

**Language.** Delivery in Arabic with English materials is the configuration most teams in Egypt and
the Gulf actually want, and it should not carry a premium — but it does require someone who can
genuinely teach in both. Translation of written materials is separate work.

**City and travel.** A programme in Greater Cairo carries no travel line. Riyadh, Jeddah, Dubai or
Abu Dhabi carry flights and accommodation, which should be quoted at cost and shown separately so
you can see exactly what the training itself costs.

**Customisation depth.** The largest hidden variable. Rebuilding exercises around your own
processes, documents and data takes preparation days that never appear on the agenda. It is also the
single thing most correlated with whether anything changes afterwards.

## What belongs in the fee, and what does not

Already included, in any serious proposal:

- The scoping conversation before the agenda is written
- Agenda design against your stated objective
- Slides, workbooks and any prompt library the participants use
- Whatever participants produce in the room — it is theirs
- A short window afterwards for follow-up questions

Legitimately separate, and always at cost:

- Flights and accommodation outside the trainer's home city
- Venue hire and catering when the session is not on your premises
- Paid tool licences the team will use during or after the programme
- Professional translation of written materials into a second language

If design, materials or the scoping call appear as billable extras, you are not buying a programme.
You are buying a presentation with options.

## Comparing two quotes without being misled

Reduce both to the same unit — total fee divided by contact hours, for the whole room. Then adjust
for the three things that change what an hour is worth:

1. **Who stands in the room.** Many providers sell on a named expert and deliver through associates.
   Ask for the name, in writing, of the person who will teach your session.
2. **The hands-on ratio.** If less than two-thirds of the time is participants working, most of the
   budget is buying a lecture.
3. **Whose data is in the exercises.** A stock case study costs the provider nothing and teaches
   your team the stock case study.

A cheaper hour taught by someone else on someone else's example is rarely the cheaper programme.

## A budgeting sanity check

Before comparing fees, price the room. Twenty managers away from their work for two days is a
significant internal cost in salary alone, and that cost is incurred whether or not the training
works. Against that, the difference between two quotes is usually small, and the difference between
training that changes behaviour and training that does not is large.

This is not an argument for the most expensive option. It is an argument for spending the evaluation
effort on what will happen in the room rather than on squeezing the fee.

## Put these in writing before you sign

- Who delivers the session, by name
- The hands-on proportion of the agenda
- Whether exercises use our data, and what happens to that data afterwards
- What participants will have produced by the end
- What is included and what is billed separately
- The invoicing entity and currency
- The lead time from signature to delivery
